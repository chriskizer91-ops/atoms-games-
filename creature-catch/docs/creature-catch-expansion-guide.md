# Creature Catch Expansion: Lore and Build Guide

**For the agent building the game (v9 and on).** This document holds everything needed to build the expansion: the lore, the new areas, the post-game story, the Lost Pages quest, every new creature, the system for the kids' creatures, buildings and interiors, and the rules for NPC dialogue.

The matching art is in `creature-catch-expansion-image-prompts.md`. Every creature, person, building, and area here has a prompt there under the same name.

**Starting point:** Creature Catch v8. Nothing here replaces the main story. The 8 trials, Juniper's fights, Lunarion, and the Moonlit Spire all stay as they are. The expansion adds side areas off the main road, a second town, a post-game arc, and 42 new creature lines.

---

## 0. Summary and Build Order

### What's new
- **Lore:** the Ancients, where the capture cube came from, and three guardians (sun, moon, and dusk) instead of one.
- **11 new places:** 9 side areas branching off the main road (including a second town, Copperbell), plus 2 post-game areas (the Dawn Temple and the Hollow Deep).
- **Post-game arc:** wake Solarion, find Gloaming, and bring all three guardians together at the Spire.
- **Lost Pages quest:** 12 pages of Elder Willow's book, *The Long Dusk*, found by talking to people and searching things in the world.
- **42 new creature lines (60 sprites):** 18 lines in existing regions, 22 in the new areas, and 2 legendaries.
- **The kids' creatures** have two homes in the lore: **Moonborn**, which can show up anywhere, and **Keepers**, epic one-of-a-kind creatures that guard each side area.
- **Every building can be entered,** and every NPC has several lines of dialogue that change as the story moves on.

### Suggested build order (one batch per session)
1. **Groundwork (no new maps):** the Lost Pages system, the NPC dialogue system, interiors for every existing building, and the new creatures in existing regions (section 6.1).
2. **Copperbell Town and Glimmer Mine:** the second town and the mine next to it.
3. **Early side areas:** Willowbrook Orchard, Whispering Grove, Driftwood Cove, Lantern Isle.
4. **Late side areas:** Steamvent Springs, Firefly Bayou, the Ancients' Archive.
5. **Post-game:** the Dawn Temple, the Hollow Deep, Solarion, Gloaming, wild creatures at the Spire, and the Balance ending.
6. **Ongoing:** the kids' creatures as they arrive (section 7).

Each batch is playable on its own. If a batch's art isn't ready yet, use the game's existing stand-in art.

## 1. Lore: The Story of the World

### 1.1 The Ancients
Long ago, people and creatures were strangers. Creatures hid in the tall grass, and people were afraid of the dark between the trees. The **Ancients** were the first people who learned that creatures weren't wild beasts, only shy.

The Ancients built the Moon Altar, the Night Ruins, the Archive under the ruins, the Dawn Temple under the desert, and the Pebbleton golems that still guard the gates. Glyphlings are runes that floated off their walls, and Clocktoise was their timekeeper.

### 1.2 Where the capture cube came from
The Ancients made the first capture cube on the shore of the great lake (today's Lakeshore) so they could carry their creature friends on long journeys.
- The **purple top** is the night sky.
- The **blue bottom** is the lake.
- The **glowing seam** is the horizon at dusk, where the two meet.

This is why the cube's seam glows, and why Mimicube can disguise itself as an "ancient cube."

### 1.3 The three guardians
- **Solarion** (Fire / Lumen), a golden winged lion, carries the sun across the sky by day.
- **Lunarion** (Lumen), the silver winged wolf already in the game, keeps the moon alight by night.
- **Gloaming** (Shadow), small and soft as smoke, is the dusk: the quiet hour each evening that is neither day nor night. It "tucks the world in." It has no light of its own, so it borrows a little from the other two.

Solarion and Lunarion never meet, because one always rises as the other sets. Gloaming carried messages between them, but it was always a little lonely.

### 1.4 The Long Dusk (what happened before the game)
1. A thousand years ago, Solarion grew tired and went to sleep beneath the desert. Its warmth made the oasis bloom. The Ancients built the Dawn Temple around it and promised to wake it when the world needed it.
2. With Solarion asleep, Gloaming had only Lunarion to visit. It clung to the moon guardian more and more until it slipped inside Lunarion's tired heart. That is the "shadow" in v8.
3. The moon dimmed. The Ancients called those dark years **the Long Dusk**. They ended it with kindness, by battling gently until Gloaming let go.
4. Ashamed, Gloaming hid deep under the earth. The Ancients sealed a door so it could rest, and carved a promise on it: *"One day, someone will come and bring you into the light."*
5. Over time, Gloaming grew lonely again and drifted back into Lunarion. The moon began to dim once more, and that is where the game begins.

### 1.5 How this connects to v8
- The game's opening ("the moon has been growing dimmer") is the second Long Dusk.
- The v8 ending line, "The shadow pours out of Lunarion like smoke and drifts away into the night," is Gloaming leaving. After the ending, it goes back to the Hollow Deep.
- Granny Moss already says she saw this happen once before, as a girl. The pages should hint that Gloaming slipped in briefly back then too, and the moon recovered on its own.
- Safi's "Moon's Eye" star stays as written.
- The Master Cube ("the ancients made it for this night") was made by the Ancients for whoever ended the next Long Dusk.

### 1.6 The Dusk Bell (Copperbell Town)
Copperbell's founders found an Ancient tradition carved in the mine: ring a bell at dusk every evening to tell Gloaming goodnight. The town has rung its **Dusk Bell** every evening for hundreds of years.

When the moon started to dim, the bell cracked and fell silent, and nobody knows why. After Gloaming is found in the post-game, the bell rings again at dusk.

### 1.7 Moonborn (where the kids' creatures come from)
The last page of *The Long Dusk* reads: *"Every full moon, the world imagines something new. A child draws a shape by lantern light, and somewhere in the tall grass a creature that never existed before opens its eyes."*

These are **Moonborn** creatures. They aren't tied to any one place and turn up anywhere. Section 7 covers how they work in the game.

### 1.8 Tone
Keep everything gentle and kid-friendly. Nobody in this world is evil. The "villain" is a lonely creature that needs a friend, and the answer is always kindness. The humor is soft: grumpy geese, a forgetful chipmunk, a sloth covered in flowers.

## 2. The World Map

### 2.1 The main road (unchanged except Copperbell)
Home Village → Meadow Path → Grassland → Forest → Lakeshore → Crystal Cave → **Copperbell Town (new)** → Sunscorch Desert → Frostpeak Pass → Murkmire Swamp → Night Ruins → Moon Altar → Moonlit Spire (post-game)

**Copperbell sits on the main road** between the Crystal Cave exit and the Sunscorch Desert. The cave's exit now opens onto Copperbell's north road, and Copperbell's south gate leads to the desert.

### 2.4 Sizes
Keep each side area small to medium, roughly the size of the Meadow Path or a bit bigger. Copperbell should be about 1.5 times the size of Home Village. The point is more places to explore, not longer walks. Each side area needs a Fly point, plus a small Healing Hut wherever a nearby one is missing (Lantern Isle, Steamvent Springs, Firefly Bayou, and the Archive's entrance).

## 3. Area Details

Each area lists its **landmark**. The landmark is the area's big visual piece and also where that area's **Keeper** waits, if a kid designs one (section 7.3).

### 3.1 Willowbrook Orchard (off the Grassland)
- **Feel:** sunny rows of apple trees, a red barn, a cider press, beehives, and a scarecrow.
- **Landmark: the Oldest Apple Tree.** A giant gnarled tree at the back of the orchard. The apples that fall near it are golden.
- **People:**
  - **Marigold,** Farmer Bramble's sister, cheerful and bossy, runs the orchard.
  - **Russet,** her son, is about Tinker's age and Tinker's best friend. He's a creature-fact know-it-all.
- **Buildings:** Marigold's farmhouse and the barn with the cider press. Both can be entered.
- **Things to find:** a hollow in an apple tree holds **Lost Page 2**. The cider press gives a free Potion once a day. Marigold sends you on a small errand: catch a Wormapple to bring her. She thinks it's eating her apples, but it actually protects them from pests.
- **Trainers:** 2 (an orchard kid and a beekeeper).
- **Wild creatures:** Wormapple (common), Puffmunk (common), Scarecrowl (uncommon, dusk and night), Bumblop (uncommon), Dandylion (rare).

### 3.2 Whispering Grove (off the Forest)
- **Feel:** huge ancient trees, shafts of light, fairy-ring mushrooms, floating lights, and soft wind sounds.
- **Landmark: the Grandmother Oak.** An enormous oak with a kind face in its bark. It talks, slowly, and remembers the Ancients.
- **People:** **Old Fennick,** a hermit in a tree house who "translates" for the Grandmother Oak. He's slightly confused about what year it is.
- **Buildings:** Fennick's tree house (can be entered).
- **Things to find:** talking to the Grandmother Oak (after Fennick introduces you) gives **Lost Page 3**. A fairy ring of mushrooms gives one rare encounter a day.
- **Trainers:** 1 (a bug-catcher kid).
- **Wild creatures:** Sprigkin (common), Mothlight (uncommon, night), Owlcorn (uncommon), Snoozeloth (rare), Budstag (rare).

### 3.3 Driftwood Cove (off the Lakeshore)
- **Feel:** sunny sand beach, tide pools, driftwood, sandcastles, a shipwreck stuck on a sandbar, and Gus's second dock with the boat to Lantern Isle.
- **Landmark: the Sandbar Shipwreck.** An old wrecked ship you can only walk out to at low tide. If the game has no tide system, it's always reachable.
- **People:** **Coral,** a beachcomber who collects shells and messages in bottles. Fisher Gus runs the boat from the dock.
- **Buildings:** Coral's beach hut (can be entered).
- **Things to find:** a message in a bottle on the beach holds **Lost Page 4**. Sandcastles you can search (some hide items). A tide pool fishing spot.
- **Trainers:** 2 (a swimmer and a sandcastle kid).
- **Wild creatures:** Shellbit (common), Splotter (common), Glimstar (uncommon, night), Tidepuff (uncommon), Crabbit (common).

### 3.4 Lantern Isle (by boat from Driftwood Cove)
- **How to get there:** after the Water trial, Gus gives you the **Boat Ticket**. Talk to him at the Driftwood Cove dock for a short boat ride (cutscene still: Boat to Lantern Isle).
- **Feel:** a small rocky island with sea cliffs, puffin nests, tide caves, and a tall striped lighthouse.
- **Landmark: the Lighthouse.** The game already has `props/landmark-lighthouse`. The lighthouse can be entered and has 3 floors. Its lamp is kept lit by a Sealamp.
- **People:** **Keeper Wren,** the elderly lighthouse keeper, sharp and funny, who has watched the sea for 50 years.
- **Buildings:** the lighthouse, Wren's cottage, and a small Healing Hut.
- **Things to find:** at the top of the lighthouse, Wren gives you **Lost Page 5**. She found it tucked in the lamp's old logbook. There's also a sea cave with a chest.
- **Trainers:** 2 (a sailor and a bird watcher).
- **Wild creatures:** Puffling (common), Bubblefin (common), Sealamp (uncommon), Krakling (rare, night), Koiling (rare).

### 3.5 Copperbell Town (main road, between the Cave and the Desert)
- **Feel:** a lively hill town of brick and copper roofs, cobblestone streets, lamp posts, a market square with a fountain, and a big copper **bell tower** in the middle. It's a mining town that grew rich on copper.
- **Landmark: the Dusk Bell Tower.** It can be entered and climbed. At the top, the big bell is cracked and silent (section 1.6).
- **Buildings (all can be entered):**

| Building | Who's inside | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Healing Hut | Nurse Holly (Hazel's cousin) | Healing and the Cube Vault |
| Cube Shop | Pim (Pip's younger brother) | Sells Great and Ultra Cubes and potions |
| Town Hall | Mayor Barnaby | Town story; the mayor worries about the silent bell |
| Museum of the Ancients | Professor Hollis | Exhibits about the Ancients; three plaques to read; gives **Lost Page 6** |
| Cubesmith Workshop | Cubesmith Ada | Shows how cubes are made; makes **Dusk Cubes** after the post-game |
| School | Ms. Poppy and 3 schoolkids | Tips on types and catching; the kids quiz you |
| Bakery | Baker Bun | Sells a "Copper Roll" that works like a Potion (just for fun) |
| Bell Tower | Old Toll, the bell-ringer | Climb to the cracked bell; Toll tells the Dusk Bell story |
| 2 houses | Townsfolk | Dialogue, small item gifts |

- **Town park:** a small garden with a patch of tall grass where wild Rustcoon and Dingdove live.
- **Things to find:** Lost Page 6 (from Professor Hollis, after you read all three plaques in the museum). The museum also gets a new exhibit after each post-game step.
- **Trainers:** 2 (in the park).
- **Wild creatures (park):** Rustcoon (common, night), Dingdove (uncommon, day).

### 3.6 Glimmer Mine (off Copperbell)
- **Feel:** an old copper mine with wooden support beams, minecart tracks, lanterns, glittering ore, flooded lower tunnels, and a huge stone door at the deepest point.
- **Landmark: the Great Geode,** a cavern-sized crystal geode partway down.
- **People:** **Grit,** a retired miner and Dusty's old partner. He closed the mine years ago because of "the cold dark behind the old door."
- **Buildings:** Grit's shack at the entrance (can be entered).
- **Things to find:** a miner's old lunchbox (a searchable object in a side tunnel) holds **Lost Page 7**. The sealed **Ancient Door** at the bottom is carved with a wolf made of smoke and the line, "One day, someone will come and bring you into the light." It stays closed until the post-game.
- **Trainers:** 2 (miners).
- **Wild creatures:** Coalcub (uncommon), Pickaxolotl (uncommon, flooded tunnels), Glimmole (common), Rumblepede (common), Shardling (rare, night).

### 3.7 Steamvent Springs (off Frostpeak Pass)
- **Feel:** a warm valley tucked in the snow, with steaming pools, bamboo fences, stone lanterns, and a cozy wooden inn.
- **Landmark: the Great Geyser.** It erupts every so often, making a rainbow in the steam.
- **People:** **Auntie Yuzu,** the warm, chatty innkeeper who runs the hot spring inn and feeds everyone soup.
- **Buildings:** the hot spring inn, which can be entered and has a bathhouse room. Resting there heals your party, like the Healing Hut.
- **Things to find:** Auntie Yuzu gives you **Lost Page 8**. She has been using it as a coaster. ("Oh, is that important? It's a lovely coaster.") A free warm-soup item once a day.
- **Trainers:** 2 (a hiker and a snowboarder).
- **Wild creatures:** Steamacaque (common), Flurrpaca (common), Sleetle (uncommon), Mittencub (rare), Yetiny (rare).

### 3.8 Firefly Bayou (off Murkmire Swamp)
- **Feel:** dark water, huge cypress trees draped in moss, thousands of fireflies, glowing lotus flowers, and stilt houses. It's magical at night and plain by day.
- **Landmark: the Lantern Tree,** a giant cypress that glows when its fireflies gather at night.
- **People:** **Boatman Bo,** a gentle, slow-talking boatman who poles a flat boat between the islands and tells legends.
- **Buildings:** Bo's stilt boathouse and a small Healing Hut. Both can be entered.
- **Things to find:** **Lost Page 9** appears only at night. The fireflies gather over a hidden stump and light it up. In the day it's just a stump. (This uses v8's day and night.)
- **Getting around:** Bo's boat moves you between 3 small islands. Each island has its own grass patch.
- **Trainers:** 2 (a bayou fisher and a firefly-catcher).
- **Wild creatures:** Fireflink (common, night), Mudtusk (common), Lotusprite (uncommon, night), Snaptrap (uncommon), Wispeel (uncommon).

### 3.9 Ancients' Archive (under the Night Ruins)
- **How to get in:** after the Ruins trial, Corvin gives you the **Archive Key** for a stairway down from the Night Ruins.
- **Feel:** an underground library lit by floating runes, with towering bookshelves, reading desks, scroll piles, a giant clockwork orrery (a model of the sun and moon), and halls of blue stone.
- **Landmark: the Forbidden Shelf,** a locked, glowing shelf at the far end that nobody can open.
- **People:** **Archivist Ilsa,** a young scholar and Corvin's niece. She is excitable and loves the Ancients.
- **Grass equivalent:** patches of glowing rune moss between the shelves.
- **Things to find:** Ilsa asks you to bring 3 Glyphlings close to a blank wall. They light up and spell a sentence, and Ilsa gives you **Lost Page 10**. Some "cubes" lying on the floor are Mimicubes (section 6.1).
- **Trainers:** 2 (scholars).
- **Wild creatures:** Glyphling (common), Inkwing (uncommon, night), Mimicube (rare), Clocktoise (rare), Pebbleton (uncommon).

### 3.10 Dawn Temple (post-game, under the desert oasis)
- See section 4 for the story steps.
- **Feel:** sandstone halls lit by shafts of sunlight, golden sun carvings, braziers, sliding stone blocks, and a great Sun Altar where Solarion sleeps curled on a pedestal.
- **Landmark: the Sun Altar.**
- **People:** **Grandmother Amani,** Safi's grandmother, who has kept the temple's secret all her life. She's the one Safi quotes about the Moon's Eye.
- **Light puzzle (simple):** turn 3 mirrors to send sunlight down the halls to open the doors.
- **Things to find:** **Lost Page 11** on the Sun Altar.
- **Trainers:** 2 (temple explorers).
- **Wild creatures:** Sphinxie (common), Mummoth (rare), Kilnadillo (uncommon), Mirajack (uncommon).

### 3.11 Hollow Deep (post-game, below Glimmer Mine)
- **Feel:** dark caverns with dim violet crystals, shadowy wisps, still black pools, and echoing whispers. Soft light is the only light. It should feel sad and quiet, not scary.
- **Landmark: the Dusk Pool,** a still pool at the very bottom where Gloaming curls up.
- **Light:** the **Sun Ember** gives a soft glow around the player, like a light circle.
- **Things to find:** after you calm Gloaming, it gives you **Lost Page 12**. It had been holding onto it all along.
- **Trainers:** none. Grit and Dusty wait at the top of the stairs.
- **Wild creatures:** Echoling (common), Umbramoth (rare), Stalagbat (common), Shardling (uncommon).

## 4. The Post-Game Arc

The arc starts after the v8 ending, once the moon is restored.

1. **The hint.** Back home, Elder Willow says pages of *The Long Dusk* are still missing, and that the shadow that left Lunarion "is still out there somewhere, alone." If the player already has 8 or more pages, Willow notices a sun symbol on page 8 and says Safi might know what it means.
2. **Safi and the oasis.** In the Sunscorch Desert, Safi waits at the oasis with Grandmother Amani. Amani opens the hidden stairs under the oasis water (cutscene still: The Dawn Temple Opens). Requirement: the moon is restored and 8 or more Lost Pages are found. If the player has fewer pages, Safi says, "Grandmother says you need to know the whole story first," with a hint about where the next page is.
3. **The Dawn Temple.** Solve the light puzzle and reach the Sun Altar. Reading page 11 wakes Solarion (cutscene still: Solarion Wakes).
4. **Waking Solarion.** A battle at Lv 48. Like the Lunarion fight, Solarion is groggy and confused, not angry. Win to calm it, then it can be caught (one per save). If it isn't caught, it waits on the Sun Altar for a retry. Either way, it gives you the **Sun Ember**: "Find the little one who hides below. Tell it it was never forgotten."
5. **The sealed door.** In Glimmer Mine, the Sun Ember melts the seal on the Ancient Door (cutscene still: The Sealed Door Opens). Grit and Dusty watch.
6. **The Hollow Deep.** Explore down to the Dusk Pool. Gloaming is curled up small (cutscene still: Gloaming in the Dark). It's frightened and tries to hide.
7. **Calming Gloaming.** A battle at Lv 50. Battle text should stay gentle, for example "Gloaming flinches away" and "Gloaming looks at you for the first time." Win to calm it, and it gives you page 12. Then it can be caught (one per save). If it isn't caught, it waits at the Dusk Pool.
8. **The Dusk Bell.** Back in Copperbell at dusk, the Dusk Bell rings again on its own, and the crack has healed (cutscene still: The Dusk Bell Rings). The museum adds a final exhibit.
9. **The Balance.** At the top of the Moonlit Spire at dusk, all three guardians appear together for the first time in a thousand years (cutscene still: The Three Guardians). They appear whether or not the player caught them. Elder Willow completes the book. Rewards: the title "Keeper of the Long Dusk," the Dusk Cube recipe at Cubesmith Ada's, and Moonborn creatures appearing twice as often (section 7.2).
10. **Credits 2.** A short second roll of the credits, including "Creatures drawn by:" with every `drawnBy` name.

## 5. Lost Pages: *The Long Dusk*

### 5.1 How it works
- **12 pages.** Each one is a key item. Reading a page opens a short storybook screen showing the page text.
- The book on Elder Willow's shelf (in `willow-in`, already in v8) shows how many pages you've found, and you can reread any page there.
- **Rewards from Elder Willow:**
  - 4 pages: 5 Great Cubes
  - 8 pages: 3 Ultra Cubes, plus her sun-symbol remark, which points to the Dawn Temple after the post-game starts
  - 12 pages: the title and the Dusk Cube recipe (section 4, step 9)
- **Pages come from a mix of sources,** so exploring and talking both pay off: 5 come from people, 5 from searching things in the world, and 2 from the post-game.
- The **Creature Log** or field menu should show a page counter, using the Lost Page icon.
- **Hints:** each area has at least one NPC who hints at where its page is.

### 5.2 Where each page is and what it says

**Page 1: "Strangers"** (Home Village: from Village Kid Tinker. He found it at the bottom of the well and gives it to you after you show him 3 different creatures you've caught.)
> In the oldest days, people and creatures were strangers. The creatures hid in the tall grass, and the people were afraid of the dark between the trees. Neither knew the other was only shy.

**Page 2: "Gifts"** (Willowbrook Orchard: search the hollow in an apple tree.)
> The first people to cross the line between field and grass were called the Ancients. They left small gifts at the edge of the meadows: apples, seeds, smooth shining stones. In the morning the gifts were gone, and a little path had been trampled through the grass.

**Page 3: "The First Friend"** (Whispering Grove: talk to the Grandmother Oak.)
> Under the oldest oak, a girl of the Ancients sat very still for a whole day. At sunset a small creature crept out and sat beside her. Neither of them moved until the stars came out. That was the first friendship, and the oak remembers it still.

**Page 4: "The First Cube"** (Driftwood Cove: a message in a bottle on the beach.)
> The Ancients wished to carry their friends on long journeys. On the shore of the great lake they shaped the first cube: purple for the night sky, blue for the water, and a seam of light where the two meet, like the horizon at dusk. A creature rests inside it as if it were home.

**Page 5: "Two Guardians"** (Lantern Isle: from Keeper Wren at the top of the lighthouse.)
> Two guardians watched over the world. Solarion, the golden lion, carried the sun across the sky. Lunarion, the silver wolf, kept the moon alight through the night. They never met, for one always rose as the other set.

**Page 6: "The Little One"** (Copperbell Town: from Professor Hollis, after you read the three museum plaques.)
> Between the day and the night there is one quiet hour, and it belongs to a third guardian, soft as smoke. The Ancients named it Gloaming. It tucks the world in for the night. It has no light of its own, so it borrows a little from each of the others.

**Page 7: "Messages"** (Glimmer Mine: search the miner's old lunchbox in a side tunnel.)
> Gloaming carried messages between the sun and the moon: a good morning from one, a good night from the other. It loved them both. But it was always the one in between, and it was always a little alone. The Ancients rang a bell each evening so it would know someone was thinking of it.

**Page 8: "The Sleeping Sun"** (Steamvent Springs: from Auntie Yuzu, who has been using it as a coaster.)
> Once the sun guardian grew tired and lay down to sleep beneath the desert sands. Its warm dreams made a spring bloom in the dunes. The Ancients built a temple around it and made a promise: when the world needs you, we will wake you. *(A golden sun symbol is drawn at the bottom of the page.)*

**Page 9: "The Long Dusk"** (Firefly Bayou: at night only, the fireflies light up a hidden stump.)
> With the sun asleep, Gloaming had only the moon to visit. It held on tighter and tighter, until it slipped inside Lunarion's tired heart. The moon grew dim, night after night. The Ancients called those dark years the Long Dusk.

**Page 10: "Kindness"** (Ancients' Archive: from Archivist Ilsa, after the Glyphling wall.)
> The Ancients did not end the Long Dusk with cubes or with anger. They battled gently, the way you play with a scared friend, until Gloaming let go. Afterward it fled deep under the earth, ashamed. The Ancients sealed a door to let it rest and carved a promise on the stone: one day, someone will come and bring you into the light.

**Page 11: "To Whoever Wakes Me"** (Dawn Temple: on the Sun Altar.)
> I am Solarion. The world turns because three of us keep it: the moon for dreaming, the sun for growing, and the dusk for resting. Find the little one who hides below. Tell it that it was never forgotten.

**Page 12: "Beginnings"** (Hollow Deep: Gloaming gives it to you after it's calmed.)
> Every full moon, the world imagines something new. A child draws a shape by lantern light, and somewhere in the tall grass a creature that never existed before opens its eyes. That is why the cube's seam glows. The world is always beginning.

## 6. New Creatures

### 6.0 Notes for building them
- **Count:** 42 new lines and 60 sprites. The game goes from 70 creatures to 130, plus the kids' creatures.
- **New-area places:** the family-creature system's `lives` field and the stat budget's region index need these new places. Suggested index `r` values (used for stats, like existing regions):

| Place | `r` |
|---|---|
| orchard | 2 |
| grove | 3 |
| cove | 4 |
| isle | 5 |
| copperbell | 5 |
| mine | 6 |
| springs | 7 |
| bayou | 8 |
| archive | 9 |
| temple | 10 |
| hollow | 10 |
| spire (wild) | 10 |

- **Signature moves:** every new line has its own special move (name, type, power, and effect). The effect uses the existing move fx keys (`status`, `stat`, `drain`, `recoil`, `crit`, `priority`, `hits`). The family-creature block only takes name, type, and power, so add the effects directly in `CC.MOVES`.
- **Time of day:** "Night" means the creature only spawns at night (or at dusk and night). "Day" means day only. "Any" means always.
- **Evolved forms** keep the first form's signature move.
- **Evolution levels** are set so no wild creature spawns at or above its evolution level.
- **Legendaries** (Solarion and Gloaming) use the existing `legend` kind and calm-and-catch flow, like Lunarion.

## 7. The Kids' Creatures

The kids' creatures go in through the existing `13b-family-creatures.js` system. Two lore categories give them a place in the world without needing a special region. A kid can also simply pick any existing area as the creature's home (`lives: 'forest'`, and so on).

### 7.1 How to choose
| If the creature is... | Make it a... |
|---|---|
| Fun or cute, and could live anywhere | **Moonborn** (section 7.2) |
| Big, powerful, or "the boss of" a place | **Keeper** (section 7.3) |
| Clearly belongs to one place (a sand creature, an ice creature) | Ordinary resident of that area (`lives:` that area) |
| An evolution for an existing Solo creature (for example, a kid wants Pengloo to have a third form) | An extra form added to that line |

### 7.2 Moonborn (can appear anywhere)
- **In the lore:** creatures the world dreamed up under the full moon (Lost Page 12). They wander everywhere.
- **In the game:** add `lives: 'anywhere'`. In every area with wild creatures, each encounter has a **3% chance** to be a random Moonborn, at that area's level range. After the Balance ending (section 4, step 9), the chance doubles to 6%, because "the moon is full again and the world is dreaming."
- **Look:** every Moonborn has a small glowing **crescent-moon mark** somewhere on its body, so players can recognize one. The image prompts add this automatically.
- **In the Creature Log:** tag the creature "Moonborn" and show its "Drawn by" credit.
- **Stats:** use the region index of the area where it's found. If one fixed set is easier, use `r = 5` (mid-game).
- **Tinker:** after you catch any Moonborn, Village Kid Tinker says, "Whoa! I drew one just like that in my sketchbook! ...Wait. Did I draw it first?"

### 7.3 Keepers (epic, one per side area)
- **In the lore:** ancient creatures who each guard one side area's landmark. Every area has one old legend about its Keeper (listed below). Keepers are bigger and more majestic than normal creatures.
- **In the game:**
  - A Keeper waits at its area's landmark as a calm-and-catch battle, like Lunarion. There's one per save, and it isn't in the spawn table.
  - Its level is the area's top level plus 6.
  - Its stat budget is `keeper`, about 420 to 460, between a final evolution and a legendary.
  - An NPC in the area tells its legend first, so players know to look.
- **Empty slots:** if no kid has designed a Keeper for an area yet, the landmark shows the legend line only and no battle starts. Nothing else depends on Keepers.
- **Look:** every Keeper has a glowing teal Ancient rune somewhere on its body (the same teal as Pebbleton's and Glyphling's runes), and its sprite is the usual 64x64, drawn to fill the whole frame (the game draws a Keeper bigger in battle itself, like every boss). The image prompts add both automatically.
- A Keeper can have an evolution or be Solo, as the kid prefers.

### 7.4 What to ask the kids for (per creature)
- A drawing
- A name
- One or two types
- Moonborn, Keeper, or a home area
- What it evolves into, if anything (a second drawing)
- A special move name
- One sentence about it
- Who drew it

The game works out everything else. Prompts that turn a drawing into a sprite are in the image prompts file (section K).

## 9. NPC Dialogue

### 9.1 Rules
1. **Every NPC has at least 3 lines.** Talking again shows the next line in order and loops back to the start.
2. **Lines change with the story.** Use story stages:
   - **Early:** before the Cave trial
   - **Mid:** from the Cave trial until the moon is restored
   - **After:** once the moon is restored
   - **Final:** after the Balance ending
   An NPC needs lines for at least two stages.
3. **Night lines:** about one NPC in three has an extra line that only shows at night.
4. **Hints:** in every area, at least one NPC hints at that area's Lost Page, one at its rare creature (and when it comes out), and one at its Keeper legend.
5. **Lead-creature reactions:** sometimes an NPC comments on your lead creature's type or name ("Is that a Pebblepup? Bramble's dog has a pebble on its nose too!"). A few per area is enough.
6. **Generic villagers and trainers get real names** (for example, "Baker Bun" or "Swimmer Kai"), and beaten trainers keep a line to say afterward.
7. **Keep lines short:** one or two sentences each, in the voice of the character.

### 9.2 Suggested data shape
```js
talk: {
  early: ['line', 'line', 'line'],
  mid:   ['line', 'line'],
  after: ['line', 'line'],
  final: ['line'],
  night: ['line'],                                    // optional, shown at night in any stage
  lead:  { Pebblepup: 'line', Fire: 'line' },         // optional, by creature name or type
}
```

## 12. Choices Made Here (Easy to Change)

These are my defaults. Ask the user before changing them, but they aren't locked.
- **Copperbell is on the main road** between the Cave and the Desert, rather than a side area. That makes it a true hub.
- **Dawn Temple gate:** 8 Lost Pages plus the moon restored. The page count could be lowered to make the post-game easier to reach.
- **Moonborn rate:** 3%, then 6% after the Balance ending.
- **Keeper level:** the area's top wild level plus 6.
- **No new trials** in side areas, so the 8 trail charms stay as they are.
- **Branching evolutions** aren't used here, since they aren't supported yet. Good first candidates if that's added later: Mimicube (Moon Shard → Mimichest, or Sun Shard → a golden version), and Fleecling (day → Cloudram, night → a dream-cloud form).
- **Tide system** for the Cove shipwreck is optional. If it's skipped, the shipwreck is always reachable.
